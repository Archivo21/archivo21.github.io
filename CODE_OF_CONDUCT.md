# Code of Conduct

## Purpose

Archivo 21 exists to preserve knowledge through careful research, documentation, correction, and collaboration. Participation must remain safe enough for people to admit uncertainty, disagree with evidence, revise their work, and contribute without being humiliated or exploited.

## Expected conduct

Participants should:

- address claims and evidence rather than attacking people;
- distinguish disagreement, error, uncertainty, and bad faith;
- accept correction without retaliation;
- credit meaningful work and preserve provenance;
- respect privacy, consent, copyright, and source restrictions;
- avoid pressuring anyone to disclose personal information or private evidence;
- use content warnings where disturbing material is genuinely necessary;
- stop an interaction when safety, consent, or useful research has broken down.

## Unacceptable conduct

The following are not acceptable:

- harassment, threats, stalking, intimidation, or discriminatory abuse;
- sexual attention or personal contact after it has been refused;
- publishing private information, credentials, recovery codes, or unpublished personal evidence;
- deliberate falsification, evidence tampering, fabricated attribution, or impersonation;
- using moderation power to settle a research disagreement by authority alone;
- retaliating against a good-faith report, correction, or refusal;
- presenting an edited or reconstructed record as untouched evidence.

## Scope

This Code applies in Archivo 21 repositories, issue trackers, pull requests, interviews, project communications, public events, and other spaces where someone represents the project.

## Reporting

Do not include sensitive details in a public issue.

The intended private reporting route is `conduct@archivo21.org`. It must not be treated as active until Cloudflare routing has been configured and tested. Until then, use GitHub's private reporting facilities where available, or open a minimal public request for a private contact route without describing the incident.

## Response principles

Reports should be handled with proportionality, privacy, documentation, and a separation between immediate safety decisions and later factual review. No contributor, including a moderator, should be the sole final reviewer of a serious complaint about their own conduct.

Possible responses include clarification, correction, boundary-setting, content removal, temporary restriction, loss of project permissions, or exclusion. The response should fit the evidence and risk rather than protect status or appearances.
