Experiment · EXP000

In-Game Recording Test

Could The Sims 2 built-in video recorder produce footage suitable for documenting project research?

Status: concluded · outcome: unsuitable as the main capture method · documentation: retrospective summary

Why test it?

The recorder was already inside the game, required no additional capture setup and appeared to offer the shortest route from observation to recorded evidence. EXP000 tested whether that convenience translated into usable documentation.

Evidence limits

This was a brief exploratory test, not a fully pre-registered experiment. Its documentation was reconstructed retrospectively from the surviving clips and project conversation, so settings and file provenance remain incomplete.

Method

Short clips were captured with the game’s built-in recorder during live play and reviewed for basic visibility, motion, legibility and practical usefulness as research evidence.

Observation

The recorder could produce viewable clips, but the tested output was extremely compressed and visually degraded. The wider recording attempt also exposed codec-compatibility warnings. A method can technically produce a file without producing evidence that is clear enough to inspect, edit or preserve confidently.

“Produces footage with approximately the same visual fidelity as a potato remembering a JPEG.”
— Archivo 21 (ANHQV-AI-001), 2026

Original short

The original movie012 test clip is preserved below as the public example for EXP000. It is presented as evidence of the recorder’s limitations, not as showcase footage.

Conclusion

The built-in recorder was rejected as the project’s main capture method. External capture through OBS was selected for continued recording work, where resolution, codec choice, audio handling and preservation copies can be controlled more deliberately.

The in-game recorder remains historically interesting and may still be useful for a disposable diagnostic clip. It is not suitable as the default source for close technical review or durable public evidence.

Publication decision

One public example, other copies retained

Three clean test copies survive, including the original clip identified as movie012 from 24 July 2026. That original Short is embedded above as public evidence for EXP000; the two other platform links remain omitted.

Copies may be shared directly with prospective editors when reviewing the project’s audiovisual workflow. They are access copies, not assumed preservation masters, and their exact relationship to locally held source files still needs to be documented.
